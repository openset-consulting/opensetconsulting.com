1:1 coaching for technical leaders

Coaching for technical leaders, in two formats.

Regular format

Leadership Coaching

For a leader taking on more than they've handled before, who wants someone to think it through with.

  • We set goals in the first month and write them down.
  • An hour together, every other week or so, spent on whatever is actually in front of them.
  • We check in on the goals periodically, but also accommodate whatever is highest on your mind that week.
  • A couple of check-ins with their manager or HR across the engagement, to align on themes and general progress.
  • Optionally, sessions are transcribed and consolidated into a progress write-up that only they see, for their own performance reviews and goal tracking.

Heavier format

The Technical Leadership Residency

For a leader holding a business initiative right now, who needs it to work.

  • One business outcome per 90 day cycle, with indicators we can measure, written up as a plan they own.
  • An hour every other week, plus reviewing real work with them (designs, plans, decisions) when it matters.
  • Async review of progress toward the outcome between sessions, and they can reach me when something breaks. Those weeks are the point.
  • At day 90, a readout to their executive: where the indicators landed and what's next.
  • Optionally, sessions are transcribed and consolidated into a progress write-up that only they see, for their own performance reviews and goal tracking.

Which one

Most people start with coaching. If a particular initiative needs more, we switch to a cycle of residency for 90 days, then back. The residency costs more because I'm co-owning a business outcome with the leader, which is more of my time and more of my risk. Everything is billed monthly, and terms renew if you want them to. The rates shown are for leaders up through the director level. Executive and C-suite engagements are scoped individually and run somewhat higher.

I'm based in Richmond, Virginia. Leaders here can meet in person. Everyone else is on video, which works fine.

In both formats, what's said in a session stays between me and the leader. Their manager gets progress against the goals, not the conversation. That's the condition that makes people honest, so I don't bend it.
